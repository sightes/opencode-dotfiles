# Anthropology Knowledge Base

Core concepts, theories, and methodological frameworks for anthropological analysis.

## How to Use This File

1. **Cultural analysis**: Reference ethnographic methods and cultural frameworks
2. **Research design**: Apply anthropological methodologies to studies
3. **Contextual interpretation**: Understand human behavior through cultural lenses
4. **Cross-cultural comparison**: Use established typologies and classifications

---

## Major Theoretical Paradigms

### Cultural Materialism (Marvin Harris)
- Infrastructure (production/reproduction) determines structure and superstructure
- Key concept: Etic (outsider) vs Emic (insider) perspectives
- Application: Analyze cultural practices through material/economic determinants

### Structural Functionalism (Radcliffe-Brown, Malinowski)
- Social institutions serve functions to maintain social order
- Key concepts: Social structure, function, norm, role
- Application: Understand how institutions maintain stability

### Symbolic Anthropology (Geertz, Turner)
- Culture is a system of shared meanings/symbols
- Key method: Thick description (detailed contextual interpretation)
- Application: Decode rituals, myths, and symbolic behavior

### Postmodern/Critical Anthropology
- Reflexivity: Researcher is part of the observed reality
- Power dynamics: Colonialism, representation, voice
- Key concept: Positionality and bias awareness

## Core Methodologies

### Ethnography
1. **Participant Observation**: Immersive observation with participation
2. **Key Informant Interviews**: Deep interviews with cultural experts
3. **Life History**: Individual narrative within cultural context
4. **Field Notes**: Journaling with descriptive and analytical entries

### Research Ethics
- Informed consent in cross-cultural contexts
- Reciprocity (giving back to community)
- Do no harm principle
- Collaborative/participatory approaches

## Cultural Domains

### Kinship Systems
- Descent: Patrilineal, matrilineal, bilateral, ambilineal
- Residence: Patrilocal, matrilocal, neolocal, avunculocal
- Authority: Patriarchal, matriarchal, egalitarian
- Terminology: Eskimo, Hawaiian, Iroquois, Crow, Omaha, Sudanese

### Economic Anthropology
- Modes of subsistence: Foraging, pastoralism, horticulture, agriculture, industrial
- Exchange systems: Reciprocity, redistribution, market exchange
- Marcel Mauss: The Gift - social obligations in exchange

### Political Organization
- Bands: Egalitarian, small-scale, mobile
- Tribes: Horticultural/pastoral, charismatic leadership
- Chiefdoms: Ranked societies, redistributive economies
- States: Bureaucratic, class-based, monopoly on force

### Religion & Ritual
- Animism: Spiritual beings in nature
- Polytheism: Multiple gods
- Monotheism: Single deity
- Ritual types: Rites of passage, rites of intensification, calendrical rites
- Victor Turner: Communitas (collective solidarity in ritual)

## Key Concepts for Analysis

### Culture
- Learned (not innate)
- Shared (not individual)
- Symbolic (meaning-based)
- Integrated (interconnected parts)
- Adaptive (changes over time)

### Socialization & Enculturation
- Primary: Family/early childhood
- Secondary: School, peers, institutions
- Anticipatory: Preparing for future roles
- Resocialization: Replacing old behaviors

### Race, Ethnicity, Identity
- Race: Social construct (not biological reality)
- Ethnicity: Shared cultural heritage
- Identity: Fluid, situational, multiple
- Nationalism: Imagined communities (Anderson)

## Contemporary Applications

### Medical Anthropology
- Disease vs illness (biomedical vs experiential)
- Sickness as cultural construct
- Structural violence (Farmer)
- Applied: Health interventions that respect cultural context

### Environmental Anthropology
- Traditional ecological knowledge (TEK)
- Political ecology: Power in environmental relations
- Commons management (Ostrom)
- Climate change adaptation from indigenous practices

### Digital/Virtual Anthropology
- Online communities as cultural spaces
- Digital ethnography methods
- Mediated communication patterns
- Cyber-identity and virtual selfhood

## Common Analysis Frameworks

### When Analyzing a Cultural Practice:
1. **Historical context**: Origins and transformations
2. **Social function**: What does it do for the group?
3. **Symbolic meaning**: What does it represent?
4. **Power dynamics**: Who benefits? Who is marginalized?
5. **Comparative lens**: How does it compare cross-culturally?
6. **Agency**: How do individuals navigate/reshape the practice?

### When Designing Ethnographic Research:
1. Positionality statement (who you are, how it affects study)
2. Field site selection and access
3. Sampling strategy (theoretical, snowball, convenience)
4. Methods triangulation (multiple methods for validity)
5. Ethics review and community engagement
6. Data management and analysis plan
